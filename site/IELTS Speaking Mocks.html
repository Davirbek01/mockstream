What is the best book you've ever read?
~30s —

Answer

I think the best book I've ever read is To Kill a Mockingbird by Harper Lee. It's a classic, and honestly, it really opened my eyes to issues like justice, racism, and empathy. The way the story unfolds through the eyes of a young girl is incredibly powerful. I picked it up in high school and couldn't put it down. What I love most is how the characters feel so real — you genuinely care about them. Plus, it taught me that "standing up for what's right isn't always easy, but it's always worth it." It's one of those books that stays with you long after you've finished reading.
2
Would you want to be an actor in the future?
~30s —

Answer

Honestly, I'm not sure. On one hand, being an actor sounds incredibly exciting — you get to step into different characters, tell stories, and connect with audiences. Plus, the idea of performing on stage or in films is really appealing. But on the other hand, it's such a competitive and unpredictable field. You have to deal with rejection, long hours, and constant public attention. I think I'd enjoy acting as a hobby, like joining a local theater group, but making it my career feels too risky. I prefer something more stable where I can still be creative but with less pressure. As they say, "Don't put all your eggs in one basket."
3
Where do you usually buy your clothes?
~30s —

Answer

I usually shop at a mix of places. For everyday stuff like t-shirts or jeans, I go to high-street stores or shopping malls because they're affordable and have a good variety. But occasionally, I browse through online stores too — it's super convenient, and you can pick up some great deals during sales. I'm not really into luxury brands; I prefer clothes that are comfortable and functional rather than flashy. Also, I try to buy from brands that are environmentally conscious when I can because sustainability matters. As they say, "Quality over quantity," so I'd rather have a few good pieces than a wardrobe full of cheap stuff.
4
What do you see in these pictures?
~45s —

Answer

Both pictures show different cultural experiences. In the first picture, I can see what looks like a lively festival — there are probably lots of people gathered together, maybe enjoying music, performances, or traditional celebrations. The atmosphere seems energetic and vibrant, with bright colors and a sense of community. Festivals are all about coming together, celebrating culture, and having fun outdoors.

In the second picture, there's a museum — probably an art or history museum with exhibits on display. The setting feels more quiet and structured. Museums are places where you slow down, observe artifacts or artwork, and learn about history or creativity. The vibe is calm and educational, unlike the festival's excitement.

So one picture represents a social cultural event — loud, interactive, full of energy. The other shows a more formal learning environment — peaceful, reflective, focused on knowledge. If I wanted to let loose and enjoy myself with friends, I'd choose the festival. If I wanted to broaden my mind and appreciate art or history, I'd visit the museum.
5
What are the advantages of attending a festival compared to a museum visit?
~30s —

Answer

I think the main advantage of attending a festival is the social experience. Festivals are incredibly lively — you get to hang out with friends, meet new people, enjoy live performances, and really let your hair down. There's this sense of freedom and celebration that you don't get in a museum. Also, festivals are usually outdoors, so you're in fresh air, moving around, dancing, or trying different foods. It's more interactive and energetic. Museums, on the other hand, are quieter and more solitary — you're mostly observing and thinking. So if you want fun, energy, and social connection, festivals are definitely the better choice.
6
Why might some people find museums boring?
~30s —

Answer

I think some people find museums boring because they can feel too formal and quiet. You're basically just walking around, reading labels, and looking at objects or paintings — there's not much interaction or excitement. For people who prefer hands-on activities or social experiences, museums can feel a bit dull. Also, if you're not particularly interested in history or art, the exhibits might not grab your attention. Museums usually require you to slow down and focus, which some people find relaxing, but others see it as boring. Plus, they can be overcrowded or poorly designed, which makes the experience less enjoyable. Personally, I think it depends on the museum and your mood at the time.
7
Travel discussion
~2:00 —

Answer

One trip that really left a lasting impression on me was when I visited Japan two years ago. It was my first time traveling abroad without my parents, so everything felt new and exciting. The moment I arrived in Tokyo, I was completely overwhelmed — the neon lights, the busy streets, the polite people, the amazing food. I spent a week exploring temples in Kyoto, trying street food in Osaka, and visiting Mount Fuji. Every day felt like an adventure. What really stuck with me was how clean and organized everything was, and how respectful everyone was to each other. I remember thinking, "This is what a well-functioning society looks like." That trip opened my eyes to how different cultures can be, and it made me appreciate both Japan's uniqueness and my own culture back home.

What made that trip so special wasn't just the sights — it was the feeling of independence and discovery. For the first time, I had to figure things out on my own: navigate the subway system, communicate despite the language barrier, manage my budget. I made mistakes, like getting lost in Shibuya or ordering the wrong dish at a restaurant, but those moments taught me to be more confident and resourceful. Also, I met people from all over the world in hostels, and we'd share stories and travel tips. Those connections made the experience feel even richer. Honestly, it wasn't about ticking off tourist spots — it was about stepping out of my comfort zone and growing as a person.

I think traveling really changes your attitude toward life and cultures. Before that trip, I had certain stereotypes about Japan from movies and anime, but experiencing it firsthand showed me how complex and beautiful real cultures are. Traveling teaches you humility — you realize your way of living isn't the only way, and that's okay. It makes you more open-minded and curious. You start appreciating small differences, like how people greet each other or what they value in daily life. Also, traveling reminds you that despite all our differences, people everywhere want similar things: happiness, connection, safety. As they say, "Travel is the only thing you buy that makes you richer." And I completely agree with that — it's an investment in yourself and your perspective on the world.
8
Art classes should be mandatory in schools — discuss both sides
~2:00 —

Answer

Some people believe that art classes should be mandatory in schools. One key argument is that art encourages creativity and self-expression. Students learn to think outside the box and express their ideas in unique ways, which is valuable in all areas of life. Additionally, art improves problem-solving skills — when students work on projects, they have to plan, experiment, and adjust their approach, which develops critical thinking. Furthermore, art classes provide a break from academic subjects like math and science. They offer a chance to relax, use hands-on skills, and engage different parts of the brain, which can actually improve overall academic performance.

On the other hand, opponents argue that not everyone is interested in art. Some students prefer sports, music, or STEM subjects, so forcing them to take art classes might feel like a waste of time. Also, making art mandatory reduces time for core subjects like mathematics, languages, and sciences, which are arguably more important for most careers. Moreover, implementing art programs can be expensive — schools need to hire qualified teachers, buy supplies, and create proper facilities, which not all schools can afford, especially in underfunded areas.

In my opinion, art classes are valuable and should be offered, but making them mandatory might not work for everyone. A better approach would be to give students a choice between different creative subjects — art, music, drama, or design — so they can explore what interests them without being forced into one specific area. This way, schools promote creativity while respecting individual preferences and managing resources more effectively.

Q1 – Best book you've ever read

🎯 Sentence starters

  • "Yes, we have quite a collection…" – "Ha, bizda ancha ko'p kitoblar bor…"
  • "We've got several shelves full of…" – "Bir necha javon to'la ... bor"
  • "My parents/family members are big readers, so…" – "Ota-onam/oila a'zolarim ko'p o'qiydi, shuning uchun…"

💬 Useful phrases / collocations

  • quite a collection – "ancha katta to'plam"
  • classics, novels, textbooks – "klassik asarlar, romanlar, darsliklar"
  • to flip through a book – "kitobni varaqlash"
  • to pick up (buy/get) – "sotib olmoq / olmoq"
  • to throw books away – "kitoblarni tashlash"
  • work on vocabulary – "so'z boyligini oshirish"

🔄 Idioms / proverbs

  • big reader – "ko'p o'qiydigan odam"
  • "A room without books is like a body without a soul." – "Kitobsiz xona – ruхsiz tanga o'xshaydi."
  • "Books are a uniquely portable magic." – "Kitoblar – ko'chma sehr."

Q4 – Picture description (festival vs museum)

  • "The second picture shows…" – "Ikkinchi rasm ko'rsatadi…"
  • "One represents [X], the other is [Y]" – "Biri [X]ni ifodalaydi, ikkinchisi [Y]"

💬 Phrases for describing

  • air travel / rail travel – "havo transporti / temir yo'l transporti"
  • taking off / flying through the sky – "parvoz qilmoq / osmonda uchmoq"
  • moving along the tracks – "yo'l bo'ylab harakat qilmoq"
  • fast, modern, exciting – "tez, zamonaviy, hayajonli"
  • grounded, calm, comfortable – "yerdagi, osoyishta, qulay"
  • grab a snack / walk around – "atıştırmalık olmoq / aylanib yurmoq"

🔄 Comparing & contrasting

  • "Planes are all about speed, trains are about enjoying the journey."
  • "If I needed to cross countries, I'd choose the plane. For a chill trip, the train."
  • "One feels rushed and stressful, the other feels relaxed and scenic."

💡 Pro Tips for Natural Speaking

  • Use linking words: moreover, furthermore, additionally, however
  • Vary your sentence structure (simple, compound, complex)
  • Include personal examples to make your speech more authentic
  • Practice stress and intonation patterns
  • Don't memorize - internalize the patterns!
